A simulation can reproduce the correct final phase fractions and still predict the wrong annealing time. Its Gibbs-energy model selects equilibrium, while its mobility model supplies a kinetic clock. Treating a database as one undifferentiated collection of “material properties” conceals that distinction.

14.1 An assessed database contains models, not just a phase diagram L31 00:21

CALPHAD, calculation of phase diagrams, is a framework for representing Gibbs energies of phases with parameterized functions and assessing their parameters against thermodynamic and phase-equilibrium evidence. A database encodes those functions, reference states, constituent models, and parameters. A phase diagram is then an output of equilibrium minimization rather than a replacement for the underlying free-energy description.

At fixed temperature \(T\) and pressure \(p\), let candidate phases be indexed by \(\alpha\). Each phase has amount \(n^\alpha\geq0\) in moles, component fractions \(N_i^\alpha\), and molar Gibbs energy \(g^\alpha\) in J/mol. Given total amounts \(b_i\), the ideal bulk equilibrium problem is

\[ \min_{n^\alpha,N_i^\alpha}\sum_\alpha n^\alpha g^\alpha(T,p,\boldsymbol N^\alpha), \qquad \sum_\alpha n^\alpha N_i^\alpha=b_i, \quad \sum_iN_i^\alpha=1. \tag{14.1} \]

Nonnegative phase amounts allow phases to disappear. Lagrange multipliers enforcing elemental balances become equilibrium component chemical potentials. Equality of those potentials between present phases recovers the common-tangent construction in a binary system. The minimization extends the same principle to many components and constituent variables. It does not include a nucleation barrier merely because a previously absent phase is allowed to acquire a positive amount.

Assessment combines phase boundaries, calorimetric enthalpies and heat capacities, chemical activities, and other compatible evidence. First-principles energies can constrain poorly measured states, especially metastable end members. Measurements have uncertainties and correlated errors; a parameter fit must therefore distinguish data selection, optimization, and independent validation. Reproducing the points used to fit a model does not establish its extrapolative accuracy.

A phase omitted from the candidate set cannot emerge from minimization. Likewise, deliberately suspending a stable phase computes a constrained metastable equilibrium rather than the unrestricted one. That can be useful when rapid processing suppresses a transformation, but the constraint must be recorded. A numerical minimum of the selected model is not automatically the physical equilibrium if model scope or candidate phases are incomplete.

14.2 Reference, ideal, and excess contributions L31 18:53

For a disordered binary substitutional phase, let \(z=N_B\). A common model separates pure reference functions, random mixing entropy, and an excess term:

\[ g=(1-z)g_A^0(T,p)+zg_B^0(T,p) +RT[(1-z)\ln(1-z)+z\ln z]+g^{\rm ex}(T,p,z). \tag{14.2} \]

The reference functions carry elemental heat capacities, entropies, and phase differences. The ideal term is a specific configurational model, not a claim that real interactions vanish. A regular solution sets \(g^{\rm ex}=L_0z(1-z)\). A Redlich–Kister representation adds composition asymmetry through \(g^{\rm ex}=z(1-z)\sum_{k=0}^K L_k(T)(1-2z)^k\), where each \(L_k\) has units J/mol. The sign convention in the odd powers depends on the chosen component ordering and must match the database.

These are functions, so their derivatives matter. A fit close in \(g\) can differ in chemical potential, curvature, or heat capacity. Parameterization must therefore be assessed against derivative-sensitive evidence as well as energies. Thermodynamic consistency connects quantities: entropy is \(-\partial_Tg\), enthalpy is \(g-T\partial_Tg\), and heat capacity is \(-T\partial_{TT}g\) at fixed pressure and composition. Fitting each independently without compatibility can produce an incoherent material model.

Multicomponent extensions combine binary and higher-order interactions. If pair terms are written as a sum over \(i<j\), each pair is counted once; summing over all unequal ordered pairs would require compensating factors. Having all binary subsystems assessed is valuable but does not fix every ternary or higher-order interaction. Eight elements generate 28 binary pairs and 56 ternary triples, as the laboratory computes. The combinatorial count describes possible subsystems, not independent proof that all are adequately measured.

Other contributions may include magnetic ordering, gas pressure, vacancies, or electronic effects. A magnetic transition can have real thermodynamic consequences even if a simplified structural phase diagram does not draw it as a separate structural phase field. A compressed caption formula is not enough to specify a magnetic model's normalization, critical temperature, and composition dependence, so no universal magnetic expression is inferred here.

14.3 Ordered phases require site fractions L33 16:46

A disordered solution assigns all substitutional atoms to equivalent sites. An ordered phase distinguishes sublattices: sets of sites that have different occupation probabilities in the ordered structure. For an illustrative B2 model with two equal sublattices, let \(y_i^{(s)}\) be the fraction of sublattice \(s=1,2\) occupied by species \(i=A,B\). Each sublattice satisfies \(y_A^{(s)}+y_B^{(s)}=1\). The overall A fraction is \(N_A=[y_A^{(1)}+y_A^{(2)}]/2\).

In a simple compound-energy description normalized per mole of atomic sites, the molar energy has end-member contributions weighted by site fractions, configurational mixing on each sublattice, and possible excess terms:

\[ g=\sum_{i,j}y_i^{(1)}y_j^{(2)}g_{i:j}^0 +\frac{RT}{2}\sum_{s=1}^2\sum_i y_i^{(s)}\ln y_i^{(s)}+g^{\rm ex}. \tag{14.3} \]

The notation \(i:j\) identifies a fully occupied end member with species i on the first sublattice and j on the second. Some end members are metastable hypothetical reference states; that does not mean they are experimentally observed phases. Sublattice ratios other than one-to-one change the entropy weights and the composition constraint. Real ordered-phase models can include vacancies, several sublattices, and interaction terms beyond this illustrative expression.

At equiatomic composition, parameterize ordering by \(\eta\in[0,1]\) with \(y_A^{(1)}=(1+\eta)/2\) and \(y_A^{(2)}=(1-\eta)/2\). The state \(\eta=0\) is disordered; \(\eta=1\) has complete A/B sublattice separation. Writing \(y=(1+\eta)/2\), the configurational entropy is \(s_{\rm conf}=-R[y\ln y+(1-y)\ln(1-y)]\). It is positive at partial order and zero only at the perfectly ordered limit of this model.

The laboratory constructs \(g(\eta)=-W\eta^2/2-Ts_{\rm conf}(\eta)\), using synthetic \(T=1000\) K and \(W=1.5RT\) for the free-energy curve in Figure 14.1. Differentiation gives \(\partial_\eta g=-W\eta+RT\operatorname{atanh}\eta\). Its nonzero minimum solves \(\operatorname{atanh}\eta=1.5\eta\), yielding \(\eta=0.858560\). The sublattice A fractions are 0.929280 and 0.070720, and the entropy remains 2.12433 J/(mol·K), compared with the disordered 5.76315 J/(mol·K). Calling a phase B2 therefore does not justify deleting configurational entropy at finite partial order.

Figure 14.1 exposes this balance between ordering energy and configurational entropy. Its free-energy curve is an original mean-field model; it is not a database assessment for a particular B2 alloy. The same representation illustrates what a phase model must specify before an equilibrium solver can minimize over its internal occupations.

14.4 A reproducible software calculation has explicit inputs L32 00:20

An equilibrium workflow first selects components, a thermodynamic database and version, candidate phases, composition variables and units, pressure, and temperature or an explicitly defined calculation axis. It then solves the constrained equilibrium problem and plots selected quantities. A Fe–C diagram plotted in mass percent is not directly comparable with one plotted in mole fraction without conversion. A plotting module does not repair inconsistent calculation conditions.

The source demonstrates graphical system definition, equilibrium calculation, and rendering, and names several CALPHAD software families. The transferable workflow is more durable than individual menu positions or license claims. This chapter makes no unsupported current comparison of commercial pricing, free editions, or package coverage. The scientific record should retain the model and input choices rather than only a screenshot of the user interface.

A transient diffusion calculation needs a second category of input. The Thermo-Calc owning DICTRA guide, in its sections on database selection, geometry, regions, boundary conditions, and thermal profiles, requires both thermodynamic and mobility databases. Geometry can be planar, cylindrical, or spherical in a single spatial coordinate. Regions carry phase selections, sizes, composition profiles, and meshes. Boundary conditions and thermal history complete the initial-boundary-value problem; the guide describes zero-flux closed boundaries as the default, which still must be checked for the chosen case.

The thermodynamic functions determine chemical potentials, phase stability, and local equilibrium relations. A mobility database supplies kinetic response functions needed to turn forces into fluxes. Selecting a thermodynamic database alone cannot determine diffusion time. A Fe–Ni homogenization demonstration therefore has both an equilibrium model and a kinetic model, as well as spatial initial data. A single nominal average composition does not specify its transient state.

A complete reproducibility record includes software version, database names and versions, component and phase lists, dependent-component conventions, geometry, region dimensions, initial profiles, boundaries, mesh policy, temperature schedule, and requested outputs. Compare predicted profiles and phase fractions with independent measurements and assess grid sensitivity. An attractive phase diagram or smooth profile certifies none of those choices automatically.

14.5 Database scope and kinetic sensitivity L33 00:20

A database's element list is only a first screening criterion. Check which phases, binary and ternary subsystems, properties, composition intervals, and temperature ranges were assessed. An interpolation within a measured binary system and an extrapolation into an unmeasured many-component alloy have different evidential support even if the software accepts both inputs. Version changes may revise phase descriptions or interaction parameters; a later diagram need not match an older demonstration exactly.

The source's thermodynamic-database file discussion distinguishes comments, element references, functions, phase definitions, constituent lists, and parameters. A parameter record is interpreted within the phase's constituent model and reference functions; its numerical coefficient is not a standalone experimental datum. Metastable end-member functions can be necessary bookkeeping. Copying isolated coefficients into another normalization can change the model silently. Database syntax, version, and dependency references are therefore part of scientific provenance.

Reassessment can alter a phase diagram when new evidence contradicts earlier assignments, as the source's Co–Sn discussion illustrates. This is a reason to preserve uncertainty and evidence lineage, rather than treating an assessed diagram as permanently exact. A machine-learning surrogate can accelerate evaluation or suggest parameters, but it must preserve mass constraints, consistent derivatives, valid constituent domains, and meaningful uncertainty outside its training support. Fast interpolation is not new thermodynamic evidence.

The kinetic distinction can be demonstrated without proprietary software. On a closed interval of length \(L\), a constant-coefficient concentration perturbation proportional to \(\cos(\pi x/L)\) satisfies no-flux boundaries. Substitution into \(c_t=Dc_{xx}\) gives amplitude \(A(t)=A(0)e^{-\pi^2Dt/L^2}\). Every positive D gives the same uniform equilibrium, but a different approach time.

For synthetic \(L=10^{-4}\) m and \(t=10^5\) s, coefficients \(10^{-14}\) and \(4\times10^{-14}\) m²/s leave amplitude fractions 0.372708 and 0.0192963. Both models conserve mean composition and eventually reach the same uniform state. Equilibrium validation alone cannot discriminate those clocks. Figure 14.1 pairs this result with the ordering-energy example because both expose information that a phase diagram alone does not supply.

14.6 Exercises with complete solutions

1. Recover chemical-potential equality

Explain how minimizing (14.1) leads to equality of component chemical potentials between present phases.

Worked solution

Introduce a multiplier \(\lambda_i\) for each elemental balance and minimize \(G-\sum_i\lambda_i(\sum_\alpha n_i^\alpha-b_i)\). An allowed infinitesimal component addition to a present phase changes this expression by \((\mu_i^\alpha-\lambda_i)dn_i^\alpha\). At an interior equilibrium this derivative vanishes, so \(\mu_i^\alpha=\lambda_i\) for each phase in which that variation is allowed. The multiplier is common across phases, giving equality. Absent phases instead obey stability inequalities; one cannot demand equality for every phase in the database.

2. Ordered does not mean zero entropy

For the equiatomic two-sublattice model, show that partial order has positive configurational entropy and identify the zero-entropy limit.

Worked solution

For \(0\leq\eta<1\), both \(y=(1+\eta)/2\) and \(1-y\) lie strictly between zero and one. Their logarithms are negative, so \(-R[y\ln y+(1-y)\ln(1-y)]>0\). At \(\eta=1\), the nonzero occupancy is one and the other is zero; with \(0\ln0=0\), the entropy vanishes. The computed partially ordered minimum at 0.858560 has entropy 2.12433 J/(mol·K). The phase name alone does not select the limiting occupancy.

3. Find the ordering threshold

Expand the synthetic ordering model near \(\eta=0\). Under what condition does that state become locally unstable?

Worked solution

The derivative is \(-W\eta+RT\operatorname{atanh}\eta\). Since \(\operatorname{atanh}\eta=\eta+\eta^3/3+\cdots\), integration gives \(g=g(0)+(RT-W)\eta^2/2+RT\eta^4/12+\cdots\). The disordered state's curvature becomes negative when \(W>RT\). This is a local mean-field threshold for the synthetic model. Real phase assessments can include asymmetry and coupling terms that alter its character.

4. Same equilibrium, different time

Derive the no-flux mode decay and explain why matching its final state does not validate D.

Worked solution

For \(c=\bar c+A(t)\cos(\pi x/L)\), the spatial derivative vanishes at zero and L. The PDE gives \(A'=-D\pi^2A/L^2\), so \(A=A_0e^{-D\pi^2t/L^2}\). Every positive coefficient drives the perturbation to zero while preserving the mean. Only transient observations constrain the decay rate. The laboratory's fourfold change in D changes the remaining amplitude from 0.372708 to 0.0192963 at the selected time without changing the limit.

5. Audit a database claim

An eight-element database contains all 28 binaries. Does that establish accurate eight-component diffusion?

Worked solution

No. Binary coverage does not determine all ternary and higher-order thermodynamic interactions, and element coverage does not establish phase-model validity at the target composition and temperature. The system has 56 possible ternary triples. Diffusion additionally requires kinetic mobilities and appropriate coupling/frame treatment. A defensible claim needs assessed scope, independent validation near the target state, version information, and uncertainty for extrapolation. Counting subsystems is an inventory check, not a predictive validation.

6. Turn a screenshot into a reproducible model

A report shows a transient concentration plot and names only a thermodynamic database. List the missing scientific inputs most consequential for reproduction.

Worked solution

The mobility database and version supply the kinetic model. The geometry, region dimensions, phases, complete initial profiles, boundary conditions, mesh, and thermal schedule specify the initial-boundary-value problem. Component units and dependent-component conventions are needed to interpret composition. Software version and selected outputs support numerical reproduction. If a phase was suspended or a seed imposed, those constraints must also be stated. A plot alone cannot identify which missing choice caused a discrepancy.
