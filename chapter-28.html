Synthesis: from a transport question to defensible evidence

A precise concentration profile can be explained by the wrong microscopic story. Completing a diffusion study therefore requires two achievements: a forward model that predicts the measured quantity, and an experiment that distinguishes that model from plausible alternatives. Precision helps only after those alternatives produce different observables.

Source spine: the course-wide revision in L60. This chapter organizes its continuum, atomistic, measurement, and materials connections around an original cumulative study. The synthetic nonidentifiability example and proposed extra observable are derived here, not reported measurements of a named material.

28.1 Start with an amount, a frame, and a boundary L60 00:20

Suppose a coating must retain a solute during an anneal. The engineering question concerns the amount remaining in a specified region after a specified time. A tracer experiment instead asks how labeled atoms spread. An electrical measurement asks how charge responds to an applied field. These tasks may involve the same atoms but require different observables and different constitutive reductions.

For a conserved molar concentration \(c\), in molm⁻³, and molar flux \(\mathbf J\), in molm⁻²s⁻¹, a stationary-frame balance is \(\partial_tc=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf J+S\), with source \(S\) in molm⁻³s⁻¹. A reaction can remove one chemical state while conserving total atoms across several states. A moving boundary can transfer inventory between regions. A good balance identifies the conserved total before adopting a single-species source term.

The constitutive law supplies additional physics. \(\mathbf J=-D\nabla c\) assumes an appropriate scalar Fickian coefficient; anisotropy requires a tensor, interacting components may require chemical-potential gradients, and an evolving structure may require time-dependent coefficients. In cylindrical geometry, radial area changes with radius. At a moving interface, the concentration jump and interface speed enter conservation. These features belong to the model, not to an after-the-fact adjustment of an inconvenient solution.

State the reference frame explicitly. A marker tracks lattice motion relative to a specimen frame; an intrinsic flux is defined relative to a chosen lattice frame. A Matano plane arises from a profile mass-balance construction under its assumptions. Neither plane is automatically the initial contact plane or the marker plane. An apparent marker displacement therefore provides additional information about flux imbalance; it does not establish that every atomic event follows a vacancy mechanism.

Boundary preparation selects the forward solution. A finite deposited amount, maintained surface concentration, prescribed flux, and impermeable surface are different conditions. An infinite-line Gaussian can approximate an internal narrow tracer layer before external surfaces matter; a half-space surface source has different normalization. A finite specimen must eventually feel its boundaries. Recording specimen size and source width makes that approximation testable.

28.2 Connect carriers to the quantity an instrument measures L60 07:30

A microscopic description supplies jump lengths, rates, and memory. For independent isotropic jumps of length \(\ell\) at total successful rate \(\Gamma\) in three dimensions, \(D=\Gamma\ell^2/6\). A per-neighbor rate differs by a coordination factor. Vacancy return or constrained ordered sequences add correlation. Equilibrium defect formation controls carrier abundance, while migration controls motion conditional on a carrier. Apparent Arrhenius and pressure derivatives can mix these contributions.

Thermodynamics supplies another part of the chain. A phase diagram constrains coexistence and solubility; it does not set a reaction-layer width or a diffusion time scale by itself. A local interface can be at equilibrium while the whole diffusion zone is evolving. The number of sequential layers in a couple is not obtained by applying one local phase-rule expression as a global layer-count theorem. CALPHAD free energies and mobility data have distinct roles, and a proprietary simulation demonstration is not reproduced merely by drawing a similar profile.

An instrument usually observes a transformed or averaged field. Serial sectioning averages across fast and slow paths. SIMS adds depth calibration, matrix response, and finite source or sputter broadening. Electron-probe resolution can smear a narrow composition gradient. Pulsed-gradient NMR encodes displacement over its gradient timing; relaxation NMR measures spectral responses related to local dynamics through a model. Mechanical loss, Mössbauer broadening, and quasielastic neutron scattering have different time and wavevector windows. Conductivity includes collective charge correlations. Coefficients inferred from these signals become comparable only through their defined forward models.

Material structure determines which alternatives deserve attention. Ordered compounds constrain chemical configurations; nanocrystals add dense, possibly evolving interfacial networks; quasicrystal symmetry constrains the tensor without uniquely identifying phason transport. Multicomponent alloy labels do not establish sluggishness. Semiconductor electronic conditions alter charged populations, while ionic neutrality couples populations of opposite effective charge. A glass can retain local order and relax during an anneal. Each observation should be interpreted within those material-specific conditions, rather than by a universal structural slogan.

28.3 A complete profile does not split a population–mobility product L60 24:51

Consider an original synthetic experiment on a chemically uniform specimen containing an internal narrow tracer layer. Work on the infinite line, in a stationary lattice frame, at fixed temperature. Assume dilute noninteracting tracer, no drift or loss, no fast-path heterogeneity, and a time-independent vacancy-mediated coefficient. Parameterize it as

\[D^*=x_VB_V,\tag{28.1}\]

where \(x_V>0\) is dimensionless vacancy fraction and \(B_V>0\), in m²s⁻¹, packages jump geometry, conditional exchange rate, and tracer correlation per unit vacancy fraction. This is a deliberately restricted mechanism model. It makes no assertion that a correlation factor is directly measured or that all vacancies in a real specimen are equivalent.

Let the initial tracer layer be a normalized centered Gaussian with standard deviation \(\sigma_0\). A centered independent Gaussian measurement response has width \(\sigma_R\). Constant-coefficient diffusion adds variance \(2D^*t\), and independent Gaussian convolution adds the response variance. Thus the observed normalized profile is

\[p_{\mathrm{obs}}(x,t)=\frac{e^{-x^2/[2s^2(t)]}}{\sqrt{2\pi s^2(t)}},\qquad s^2(t)=\sigma_0^2+2x_VB_Vt+\sigma_R^2.\tag{28.2}\]

Here \(p_{\mathrm{obs}}\) has units m⁻¹ and integrates to one; multiplying by a conserved areal tracer amount gives a concentration. The zero mean and full-line geometry are part of the construction. Real source or response shapes need not be Gaussian, but their calibration is still necessary.

Model A has \(x_V=10^{-6},B_V=10^{-10}\)m²s⁻¹. Model B has \(x_V=10^{-4},B_V=10^{-12}\)m²s⁻¹. Both give \(D^*=10^{-16}\)m²s⁻¹. With \(\sigma_0=20\)nm, \(\sigma_R=15\)nm, and \(t=3600\)s, both give \(s^2=7.20625\times10^{-13}\)m² and observed width0.848896µm. Their entire observed profiles agree, not merely a fitted slope or second moment.

More generally, replacing \(x_V\) by \(a x_V\) and \(B_V\) by \(B_V/a\), for any positive factor that keeps the dilute model admissible, leaves every profile at every time unchanged. This proves structural nonidentifiability of the two factors from these tracer profiles. An anneal-time series can separate the slope \(2D^*\) from the fixed source-plus-response intercept. It still cannot split \(x_VB_V\).

The laboratory uses900,1800,3600,7200s and recovers \(D^*=10^{-16}\)m²s⁻¹ together with intercept \(6.25\times10^{-16}\)m². That verifies profile evolution and broadening bookkeeping under the assumed model. It does not tell whether the observed coefficient came from few highly mobile defects or many less effective defects. The distinction is the experimental target of the next section.

28.4 Choose an observable with a different parameter dependence L60 21:47

Suppose a separately calibrated defect measurement gives dimensionless signal \(y=\kappa x_V\) with known positive calibration \(\kappa\). Differential dilatometry or a calibrated trapping measurement, developed in Chapter6, can motivate a population constraint, but each needs its own response model, defect specificity, and saturation checks. The linear signal here is a synthetic idealization; it is not a universal positron or volume law.

Set synthetic \(\kappa=0.5\). Models A and B predict signals \(5\times10^{-7}\) and \(5\times10^{-5}\), respectively. If a measurement resolves these values and the calibration applies to the same defect population present during annealing, it distinguishes the models. Inversion becomes \(x_V=y/\kappa\) and \(B_V=D^*\kappa/y\). If the specimen is quenched for measurement, defect survival and redistribution during the quench require verification.

The information gain is visible algebraically. Define dimensionless logarithmic parameters \(a=\ln x_V\) and \(b=\ln[B_V/B_{\mathrm{ref}}]\), with fixed positive reference \(B_{\mathrm{ref}}\) in m²s⁻¹. The tracer log observable \(\ln[D^*/B_{\mathrm{ref}}]=a+b\) has sensitivity row \((1,1)\). The independent signal log \(\ln(y/\kappa)=a\) has row \((1,0)\). Together they give

\[\mathsf J=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det\mathsf J=-1.\tag{28.3}\]

The single-observable Jacobian has rank one; the augmented matrix has rank two. This establishes local identifiability of these two positive parameters within the chosen model. It does not establish that every alternative microscopic model has been eliminated. If the calibration \(\kappa\) is also unknown, another parameter enters and the ambiguity can return. Measurement noise and weak sensitivity create practical uncertainty even when rank is formally full.

The same design principle explains the complementary measurements in Table28.1. A C-regime experiment constrains boundary mobility separately from a B product; electrical activation constrains a dopant inventory different from chemical total; charge transport and tracer displacement contain different correlations. A useful extra experiment changes parameter dependence. It should be selected from the ambiguity in the model, not merely because another instrument is available.

28.5 A cumulative study has explicit rejection tests L60 28:57

For the synthetic study, first characterize phase identity, grain and dislocation structure, source width, specimen dimensions, and instrument response. Then measure a time series on equivalently prepared specimens, checking conserved tracer amount and linear variance growth. Persistent non-Gaussian tails, changing phase, or time-dependent slope reject the simple homogeneous stationary model and direct attention to leakage, trapping, or relaxation. A good residual is necessary but cannot identify factors that enter only through one product.

Next obtain the independent population observable under conditions linked to the anneal. Fit both observables jointly, propagate calibration uncertainty, and test whether one parameter set predicts all times and conditions. Changing temperature, electronic state, sink density, or pressure can supply further constraints, but each intervention may also change the mechanism or structural state. A temperature sweep across a phase transition cannot be treated as a clean perturbation of one constant model.

The scientific report should preserve input units, preparation, measured signals, forward equations, fit domain, residuals, and alternatives not excluded. Original research is needed for empirical mechanism claims; the source revision's bibliography is not reconstructed from unread slide text. Earlier chapters integrate the inspected primary sources beside their supported claims. The reproducible laboratory and saved output complete the synthetic study with equal-profile, time-series, and rank checks. They establish the stated mathematical example, not an experimental material result.

28.6 Cumulative exercises and worked solutions

  1. Perfect data. Would exact profiles at every time determine both parameters in (28.1)?

    Solution

    No. The transformation \((x_V,B_V)\mapsto(ax_V,B_V/a)\) leaves their product and hence every forward profile unchanged. Exact observations remove statistical uncertainty in the product but leave a continuum of parameter pairs. This is structural nonidentifiability. It differs from a poorly resolved estimate that could improve with more precise data of the same kind.

  2. Resolution bias. If one profile is inverted using a zero-width source and perfect instrument despite the stated nonzero widths, what coefficient is inferred?

    Solution

    The naive estimate is \(D_{\mathrm{naive}}=s^2/(2t)=D^*+(\sigma_0^2+\sigma_R^2)/(2t)\). It overestimates diffusion. The bias decreases with anneal time for constant widths. A time-series slope separates this fixed intercept from diffusion, but cannot by itself distinguish initial width from instrument width. Zero-time and response calibrations address those separate terms.

  3. Unknown calibration. Suppose \(\kappa\) is unknown. Explain why adding the population signal can leave an ambiguity.

    Solution

    There are now three unknown positive parameters with observables \(D^*=x_VB_V\) and \(y=\kappa x_V\). The transformation \(x_V\to ax_V,B_V\to B_V/a,\kappa\to\kappa/a\) leaves both unchanged. A separate calibration or another independent observable is required. An instrument's sensitivity is not equivalent to known parameter specificity.

  4. Model rejection. A tracer second moment is linear in time, but the profile has persistent deep tails. Does the moment validate the Gaussian model?

    Solution

    No. Many non-Gaussian distributions share the same second moment, and mixtures can have linear variance. Compare the full profile with the instrument-convolved forward prediction and test tail reproducibility, amount conservation, and microstructural localization. A homogeneous Gaussian model predicts all its shape, not only its variance. The tail may motivate fast paths or multiple populations, but its existence alone does not uniquely select either.

  5. A research claim. Formulate a defensible conclusion after the two observables agree with Model A and reject Model B.

    Solution

    State that, within the homogeneous dilute vacancy model and the calibrated population-response assumptions, the measured tracer coefficient and independent signal support the lower-population/higher-mobility parameter set over the specified alternative. Report uncertainties and the annealing domain. Do not conclude that every possible defect geometry or microscopic correlation model has been excluded. New observables would be needed to decompose the mobility factor or challenge the common mechanism assumption itself.

A complete diffusion argument connects conserved inventory, carrier statistics, constitutive law, boundary preparation, instrument response, and identifiable parameters. The final scientific product is a conditional explanation with explicit tests that could overturn it.

"""Cumulative inverse problem: identical profiles, different carriers."""

from pathlib import Path
import json
import numpy as np
import matplotlib
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

matplotlib.use("Agg")
matplotlib.rcParams["svg.hashsalt"] = "ch28"
ROOT = Path(__file__).resolve().parents[1]


def main():
    fraction = np.array([1e-6, 1e-4])
    mobility = np.array([1e-10, 1e-12])
    diffusion = fraction * mobility
    assert diffusion[0] == diffusion[1]
    initial_width, resolution, time = 20e-9, 15e-9, 3600.0
    observed_var = initial_width**2 + 2 * diffusion * time + resolution**2
    coordinate = np.linspace(
        -5 * np.sqrt(observed_var[0]), 5 * np.sqrt(observed_var[0]), 1201
    )
    profiles = np.exp(
        -(coordinate[None, :] ** 2) / (2 * observed_var[:, None])
    )
    profiles /= np.sqrt(2 * np.pi * observed_var[:, None])
    times = np.array([900.0, 1800.0, 3600.0, 7200.0])
    variances = initial_width**2 + resolution**2 + 2 * diffusion[0] * times
    slope, intercept = np.polyfit(times, variances, 1)
    coefficient = 0.5
    independent_signal = coefficient * fraction
    profile_J = np.array([[1.0, 1.0]])
    augmented_J = np.array([[1.0, 1.0], [1.0, 0.0]])
    out = {
        "status": "synthetic identifiability construction; no material fit",
        "models": [
            {
                "vacancy_fraction": float(v),
                "mobility_factor_m2_s": float(b),
                "tracer_D_m2_s": float(d),
                "independent_signal": float(y),
            }
            for v, b, d, y in zip(
                fraction, mobility, diffusion, independent_signal
            )
        ],
        "at_3600s": {
            "observed_variance_m2": float(observed_var[0]),
            "observed_width_m": float(np.sqrt(observed_var[0])),
            "profile_max_difference": float(
                np.max(abs(profiles[0] - profiles[1]))
            ),
        },
        "time_series": {
            "times_s": times.tolist(),
            "variances_m2": variances.tolist(),
            "recovered_D_m2_s": float(slope / 2),
            "recovered_initial_plus_resolution_var_m2": float(intercept),
        },
        "log_parameter_ranks": {
            "profile_only": int(np.linalg.matrix_rank(profile_J)),
            "augmented": int(np.linalg.matrix_rank(augmented_J)),
        },
    }
    assert np.isclose(slope / 2, diffusion[0], rtol=1e-12, atol=0)
    assert np.max(abs(profiles[0] - profiles[1])) == 0
    fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(9, 4))
    axes[0].plot(coordinate * 1e6, profiles[0] * 1e-6, label="Model A")
    axes[0].plot(coordinate * 1e6, profiles[1] * 1e-6, "--", label="Model B")
    axes[0].set(
        xlabel="Depth coordinate (µm)", ylabel="Normalized density (µm⁻¹)"
    )
    axes[0].legend()
    axes[1].bar(["A", "B"], independent_signal)
    axes[1].set(ylabel="Synthetic independent vacancy signal", yscale="log")
    for ax in axes:
        ax.grid(alpha=0.25)
    fig.tight_layout()
    fig.savefig(
        ROOT / "assets/ch28-identifiability.svg", metadata={"Date": None}
    )
    plt.close(fig)
    (ROOT / "code/ch28-output.json").write_text(
        json.dumps(out, indent=2) + "\n"
    )
    print(json.dumps(out, indent=2))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
