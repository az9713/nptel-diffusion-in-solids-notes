Foundations and inverse transport

  • Fick (1855), On liquid diffusion. Publisher bibliographic metadata supports the historical attribution; no complete historical-paper reading is claimed.
  • Darken (1948), original scan. Original printed pp.185–193, especially p.188 Eqs.7–9 and p.192 Eq.18, support the constant-volume frame and mobility discussion. The scan’s opening commentary is a separate later work.
  • Tas Kavakbasi and colleagues (2017), inverse-profile analysis. Inspected equations and fitting comparisons support the discussion of derivative/tail sensitivity; a specific fit is not a universal estimator.

Software and measurements

  • Thermo-Calc, Diffusion Module getting-started guide. Owning workflow documentation for thermodynamic/mobility databases, geometry, initial profiles, boundary conditions, and thermal history.
  • NIST magnetic-sector SIMS program. Technique and calibration context.
  • Stejskal and Tanner (1965). The inspected original gradient-timing derivation supports the pulsed-gradient diffusion attenuation model.
  • BIPM DDEP Co57 evaluation, Table2. Evaluated Fe57 level half-life, distinguished from mean lifetime in linewidth calculations.
  • Mantid resolution-convolution documentation. Owning documentation for the measured spectrum versus intrinsic model distinction.

Material-specific transport

  • Vaidya and colleagues (2017), high-entropy-alloy grain-boundary diffusion; published paper. Inspected B/C analysis, Table1, and comparisons support a scoped counterexample to universal component-count rankings.
  • Morgan (2017), lattice-geometry effects in garnet solid electrolytes. Inspected equations distinguish single-particle and collective correlation factors. Garnet numerical factors are not transferred to unrelated ionic lattices.
  • Nichols, Van de Walle, and Pantelides (1989), semiconductor dopant diffusion mechanisms. Publisher abstract access only; no uninspected full-paper formula is attributed to it.
  • Ruta and colleagues (2012), metallic-glass relaxation and aging. Scoped primary context for thermal-history-dependent relaxation.
