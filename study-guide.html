Study guide and learning outcomes

Use each chapter’s derivations and worked exercises before running its code. Record the conserved quantity, frame, initial state, boundary data, and observation model before selecting a diffusion coefficient.

  1. Conservation, gradients, and Fick's laws

    For fixed geometry with no reaction, partial_t c = -partial_x J; with J=-D partial_x c, partial_t c=partial_x(D partial_x c).

    Check: finite-volume conservation, layered-slab resistance, radial profiles, and rotated tensors.

  2. Laplace transforms and diffusion initial data

    Under stated regularity and convergence assumptions, D C_xx-s C=-c(x,0), where C is the one-sided time transform.

    Check: ODE residuals, initial data, inverse-transform coefficients, and transformed PDE units.

  3. Gaussian and error-function diffusion solutions

    An instantaneous infinite-domain plane source of areal amount M has c=M exp(-x²/(4Dt))/sqrt(4 pi Dt).

    Check: normalization, second moment, error-function boundary limits, uptake and inverse-profile recovery.

  4. Finite domains and reproducible diffusion computation

    For the source-free constant-D uniform-grid explicit interior update, 0<=D dt/dx²<=1/2 makes each new cell value a convex average; positivity of the whole scheme also requires positivity-preserving boundary updates.

    Check: positivity, mass conservation, refinement against an exact solution.

  5. Crystal geometry, sublattices, and defect bookkeeping

    A crystal defect count requires a specified sublattice and site multiplicity; equal defect numbers need not imply equal site fractions.

    Check: site counting, charge/stoichiometric balance, and geometry.

  6. Vacancy populations, binding, and defect measurements

    Independent vacancy sites give x_v/(1-x_v)=exp(-g_f/(k_B T)); dilute favorable pair binding gives x_2v=(z/2)exp(g_b/(k_B T))x_1v² with explicit counting conventions.

    Check: free-energy minimum, missing-site balance, differential dilatometry and trapping-model identifiability.

  7. Random walks and atomistic diffusion mechanisms

    Independent isotropic jumps of length ell and total rate Gamma in d dimensions give D=Gamma ell²/(2d).

    Check: exact walk moments and seeded ensemble simulation.

  8. Correlation factors and tracer memory

    For stationary zero-mean fixed-length jump increments with absolutely summable correlations, f=1+2 sum_{k>=1} E(step_0 dot step_k)/ell²; the finite-step factor includes weights 1-k/n.

    Check: one-dimensional persistent-walk exact moments.

  9. Temperature, pressure, and activation parameters

    For a single Arrhenius mechanism with temperature-independent prefactor, d ln D/d(1/T)=-Q/R.

    Check: synthetic parameter recovery and two-path curvature.

  10. Free energy, interdiffusion, and phase formation

    For binary molar free energy g(N_B), mu_B-mu_A=dg/dN_B; a common tangent equates both component chemical potentials.

    Check: regular-solution curvature and tangent conditions.

  11. Reading composition profiles and diffusion paths

    A sharp locally equilibrated interface permits a concentration jump; conservation requires v(c_right-c_left)=J_right-J_left.

    Check: interface mass balance and scaling.

  12. Boltzmann–Matano analysis as an inverse problem

    For an isothermal single-phase semi-infinite step with constant molar volume, no reaction or short-circuit heterogeneity and a strictly monotone smooth similarity profile, D(c*)=-[1/(2t c_x)] integral_{c_left}^{c*}(x-x_M)dc at interior nonzero-slope points; x_M is fixed by the mass-balance area condition.

    Check: recover a constant diffusivity and quantify noise sensitivity.

  13. Kirkendall markers, intrinsic fluxes, and Darken relations

    For constant V_m and binary intrinsic diffusivities, D-tilde=N_A D_B+N_B D_A; marker velocity v_K=V_m(D_B-D_A) partial_x c_B.

    Check: independent frame-transformation calculation.

  14. CALPHAD thermodynamics and kinetic databases

    Thermodynamic functions determine driving forces; mobilities determine rates, so equilibrium data alone do not identify diffusion time scales.

    Check: same equilibrium with distinct kinetic trajectories.

  15. Moving boundaries and multiphase simulation

    For a quasi-steady layer with fixed concentration drop and partition jump, thickness squared grows linearly with time under stated sign and geometry conventions.

    Check: mass balance and an independent interface trajectory.

  16. Tracer profiles, SIMS, and electron-probe measurements

    On the infinite line, a normalized Gaussian initial profile under constant-D diffusion has variance sigma_0²+2Dt; convolution with an independent centered Gaussian response adds its variance. A half-space surface source uses a different centered-variance convention.

    Check: recover D with and without instrumental broadening.

  17. Mechanical relaxation and electrical diffusion probes

    For a single Debye relaxation, the loss peak occurs at omega tau=1; its activation fit uses ln omega versus 1/T_peak.

    Check: loss-peak sweep and independent Gorsky time ratios.

  18. NMR diffusion measurements and local jump times

    For the stated free-diffusion pulsed-gradient sequence, echo attenuation is exp[-gamma² g² delta² D(Delta-delta/3)].

    Check: synthetic attenuation fit and timing/units sensitivity.

  19. Mössbauer and neutron scattering probes

    Exponential free-diffusion self-correlation gives angular-frequency HWHM D q²; energy HWHM is hbar D q².

    Check: Fourier-transform linewidth and q-dependent jump model.

  20. Grain-boundary diffusion and kinetic regimes

    Boundary transport depends on both fast along-boundary diffusion and leakage into adjoining grains; the relevant bulk penetration scale is sqrt(Dt).

    Check: regime length-scale comparison and two-region conservation.

  21. Dislocation pipe diffusion and pathway competition

    For parallel connected pathways sharing a gradient and local equilibrium, D_eff=sum_i phi_i D_i; this arithmetic average fails when exchange or connectivity is limiting.

    Check: path-volume sensitivity and series/parallel counterexample.

  22. Ordered, nanocrystalline, and quasicrystalline materials

    A larger interfacial volume fraction increases a simple parallel-path coefficient only if interfacial diffusivity, connectivity, and exchange assumptions remain fixed.

    Check: grain-size geometry and model sensitivity.

  23. Diffusion mechanisms in metallic alloys

    A measured diffusivity reflects pathway populations, jump mobilities, correlations and reference conditions; no single material label determines its ordering.

    Check: trap-limited transport, defect-population competition and controlled rate comparisons.

  24. High-entropy alloys and the evidence for diffusion claims

    A diffusion ranking requires named species, phase, coefficient, temperature convention and comparable microstructure; high configurational entropy alone proves no universal kinetic ordering.

    Check: same-absolute versus homologous-temperature ranking and primary-data audit.

  25. Defect-mediated diffusion in semiconductors

    The semiconductor diffusion coefficient depends on the populations and mobilities of participating defects; a single constant-D profile is a restricted process model.

    Check: charged-defect population and mobility sensitivity, source/sink nonequilibrium, and explicit tutorial measurement limits.

  26. Diffusion and structural relaxation in metallic glasses

    For spatially uniform D(t), the diffusion equation reduces to constant-coefficient form under accumulated diffusion time tau=int_0^t D(s) ds.

    Check: equal final profiles from distinct time histories.

  27. Ionic solids, defect chemistry, and coupled charge transport

    For independent dilute carriers of charge q and number concentration n, conductivity=n q² D/(k_B T); tracer-to-charge correlation and multiple carriers require qualification.

    Check: neutrality and mass-action solutions, conductivity-minimum location, intrinsic/extrinsic crossover, and tracer-versus-charge correlation.

  28. Synthesis: from a transport question to defensible evidence

    Identical concentration profiles can arise from different microscopic mechanisms; mechanism inference requires additional observables or constraints.

    Check: construct an explicit nonidentifiability example and experimental remedy.
